# Fairness, guard progress and model composition

These arguments apply to the current checker source. Historical counts below
refer to the packed guard before the optional polling edge; current guard
counts are in `guard-v5-totals.json`.

## Fair-cycle criterion and time abstraction

Strong actor fairness requires each actor enabled infinitely often to execute progress infinitely often. The recurrent states of any infinite finite-graph execution form a strongly connected set. An SCC can support a fair closed walk when every actor enabled at any vertex has an internal progress edge. If an enabled actor has no such edge, every fair subset must omit its enabling vertices. The checker removes those vertices and recomputes SCCs instead of rejecting the entire component. Witness construction includes every required actor edge, using internal connecting paths.

Threshold events use weak time fairness (justice): a threshold process enabled continuously must run eventually. A traffic actor may complete and restart waits before any reaches the threshold. Age masks are enabled only after first enqueue while the same operation remains pending, and are disabled at operation boundaries. The witness includes an age edge or a vertex disabling each potentially continuous process. A registered uncancelled victim's age flag cannot reset before ownership or cancellation; a recurrent registered wait therefore must include an aged state.

The eligibility metadata is true before first registration although the physical age flag is false there. No threshold event is enabled before registration. This permits checking pre-registration livelocks without inventing elapsed waiting time. `VictimActive` includes every uncancelled pending operation from entry through registration, queue wait, self-send, and guarded cleanup that can precede ownership. Numerical phase order does not determine whether a pending waiter may age.

The generic analyzer is checked against an independent recurrent-subset oracle for all 40,000 two-vertex graph/metadata combinations and 2,000 reproducible random graphs with at most seven vertices. Named tests cover hidden fair subcomponents, repeated pruning, intermittent enablement, justice, victim exclusion, malformed inputs, bit 15, zero-actor stutters, and a 20,000-node directed ring. The retained `all-engine-mutations.json` lists changed source text, commands and assertion outcomes for the fairness and guard controls.

## Guard proof and composition

The internal guard has one atomic 64-bit word. The upper 32 bits contain the next unallocated ticket and the lower 32 bits contain the current head. A blocking claim adds `1<<32` and remembers the old upper half as its ticket. A caller enters the guarded body only after a whole-word load observes that its ticket equals the head. A successful Try reads an idle whole word and atomically increments only its upper half with a whole-word CAS. Unlock loads the word and CASes a replacement with only the lower half incremented modulo 2^32; head wrap cannot carry into the tail.

Assume fewer than 2^32 outstanding guard claims and that each guarded body finishes after finitely many of its owner's steps. Outstanding tickets are then distinct. Only the current head claimant can enter, and only that claimant advances the head. A head claimant polling forever is continuously enabled to load the unchanged matching head and enter; strong actor fairness schedules it. Each earlier ticket therefore completes before a later ticket reaches the head. New claims receive tickets behind all outstanding claims and cannot overtake them.

Unlock's full-word CAS can lose to concurrent tail allocations. While the head owner is releasing, the head remains fixed. Each competing blocking caller allocates at most one ticket and then waits for a different head. Try sees a busy head/tail pair and cannot allocate. Under the outstanding-claim bound, only finitely many tail changes can interfere before this release succeeds. This finite-interference argument is essential: the replaced queue registration CAS could lose to repeated fast Lock/Unlock operations without increasing outstanding claims.

The model uses three-bit counters with at most four outstanding actors. It models allocation, head load, comparison, yield, guarded body, release load/CAS, Try load/CAS, and operation restart as separate steps. Relative normalization subtracts the current head from counters, tickets, and saved snapshots modulo eight; it preserves every equality and CAS comparison, including stale snapshots and wrap. Thirty bounded method assignments cover one through four actors. Fourteen continuous traffic assignments cover two through four actors, with a blocking victim and all combinations of repeating blocking/Try traffic. The extra packed-Try wrap graph has 125 states and 242 edges; explicit boundary tests seed both halves at wrap.

The separate-counter design is unsafe even below the outstanding-claim bound. A stale Try head load of zero can survive seven completed tickets and a new ticket-seven claim, after which tail wraps to zero. Tail-only CAS succeeds and lets the stale Try own a future ticket while the rightful head claimant can also enter. The preserved negative graph has 93 states and 176 edges; its 40-transition replay ends with two simultaneous owners. Packing both halves makes that busy word fail the stale CAS. The full packed word can return to its original idle value only after all prior claims finish, at which point the same CAS safely claims the current idle head.

`qTake` and RW guard acquisition abstract successful short-section admission. They permit more orders than the FIFO ticket implementation. Internal polling may add stuttering, but the guard proof makes each admitted head body and release finite under the same actor fairness. Contracting those finite guard stretches yields an execution permitted by the model. Queue accounting bodies have bounded operations after iteration 3. Reader batch release is finite because its queue is finite when the guard is acquired and other actors cannot extend it while that guard is held. The composition therefore does not assume starvation freedom of an unexamined standard-library mutex.

## Required product mapping

Fast Mutex Lock/Try CAS and Unlock Add change only the held bit. Count, normal wake marker, and starvation mode change under the queue guard. A slow eligible acquisition attempts one CAS; failure proceeds to a fixed registration Add. Registration enqueues before releasing the guard and proactively advances the queue only when its Add result observed no owner. Cancellation, normal wake reservation, and starvation adoption use fixed deltas that preserve concurrent held-bit changes. Normal release/cancellation wake may grant competition even when a barger is already holding the lock; it does not grant ownership. A registering sender that wakes itself or an older waiter resumes its own pending wait after the separate buffered send.

RWMutex uses the gated Mutex for writer serialization. A short RW guard protects the active reader count, reader queue, writer announcement, and writer drain signal. Counts include granted reader reservations before signal delivery. Fresh readers queue after a pending writer announcement. Writer release or cancelled-announcement rollback grants queued readers before releasing writer serialization. A granted reader cancellation decrements exactly its reservation; only the transition to zero emits the current writer's drain signal. The writer may own the public lock only after all admitted readers finish.

RW negatives fail as required: forgotten readers after writer cancellation (6,563 states / 20,728 edges, 25-transition P3 trace), reader undo count error (2,061 / 4,739, 21-transition P1 trace), and reader admitted past a pending writer (47 / 73, 9-transition P3 trace). Queue named controls are in `queue-v6-prior-named-controls.json`; the seven accounting/age controls are in [queue-v3-mutations.json](queue-v3-mutations.json). The exit-age control uses a seeded policy assertion; its purpose is the exact exit condition, not an additional reachable SCC witness.

The model does not prove arbitrary actor counts by enumeration, real scheduler fairness, performance, memory exhaustion behavior, or claims beyond the outstanding ticket capacity. The guard arithmetic and rank argument address general finite ticket counts below capacity; the public finite graphs exercise up to four actors and two operations, with three actors in continuous traffic. Runtime tests, race checking, allocation checks, benchmarks, and full-repository validation are separate evidence.
